Federated Learning: A Privacy-Preserving Approach in AI

Review Paper, University of Asia Pacific - 2025

A review of federated learning for secure and efficient data exchange across industries and e-commerce while reducing the need to share raw data.

Federated Learning (FL) has emerged as a disruptive paradigm in artificial intelligence, enabling collaborative training of models on decentralized devices while keeping data where it is generated. Unlike traditional centralized AI systems that collect and anonymize data in the cloud, FL preserves confidentiality by ensuring that raw data never leaves the user's device. In addressing privacy, security, and regulatory compliance concerns, FL offers strong advantages for sensitive data. This review presents a comprehensive overview of privacy-preserving techniques in Federated Learning, including secure aggregation, differential privacy, homomorphic encryption, and secure multi-party computation. The study examines major FL paradigms, covering system architectures, optimization strategies, adversarial threats, and real-world applications such as healthcare analytics, financial modeling, and Internet of Things (IoT) environments. Additionally, it discusses core challenges like communication efficiency, non-IID data, and system heterogeneity. Finally, the review outlines future research directions to enhance the robustness, scalability, and personalization of FL systems. This work contributes to the growing discussion on FL, emphasizing its importance as a foundational technology for building trustworthy and privacy-aware AI systems.
